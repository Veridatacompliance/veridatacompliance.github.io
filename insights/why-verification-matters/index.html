Perspective

Why verification deserves its own step

Gathering information and confirming it are related tasks, but they ask for different habits of mind. We think the work of checking deserves dedicated time and attention.

Gathering and checking ask different questions

When information is being collected, the natural focus is on breadth: finding what exists, following promising threads, and building a picture that feels reasonably full. That momentum has value, but it tends to reward progress over scrutiny. Checking asks a quieter question, which is whether each piece actually says what it appears to say.

In our view, these modes of work pull in different directions. Folding them together can mean that verification happens in passing, shaped by whatever was easiest to confirm in the moment. Giving it a separate step creates room to slow down, look again, and treat each finding as something to be tested rather than simply accepted.

Returning to the source

A summary of information is not the same as the information itself. Details can shift as they are restated, condensed, or combined with other material, often without anyone intending it. Returning to the original source is a simple discipline, but it is easy to skip when time is short and a restated version seems close enough.

We seek to trace each meaningful point back to where it came from and to confirm that it reads the same way there. Where that is not possible, we think the right response is to say so directly, so that the reader understands which findings rest on direct confirmation and which rest on something less certain.

Gaps and conflicts are findings too

It can be tempting to treat missing information or conflicting versions as obstacles to work around. We see them differently. A gap shows where the available picture ends, and a conflict shows where reasonable sources disagree. Each is meaningful, and each can shape how a client chooses to read the rest of the work.

Noting these points takes deliberate effort. It means writing down what was looked for and not found, marking where versions differ, and resisting the urge to quietly choose a preferred answer. Our approach is to bring these details to the surface so that the people relying on the work can weigh them for themselves.

Candor about limits builds confidence

Research of any kind has limits. Sources can be incomplete, dated, or open to differing readings, and time and scope place natural boundaries on any engagement. Acknowledging those limits openly does not weaken the work. In our view, it gives the reader a firmer sense of how much weight each conclusion can bear.

That is why we treat verification as a step with its own time and attention rather than a final glance before reporting. When checking is given room, the resulting report can be more measured, more transparent, and more practical as a basis for informed decisions. We believe that is worth the added effort.

Key points

  • Gathering and checking call for different habits, and each benefits from its own dedicated time.
  • Gaps and conflicts are worth noting openly, since they shape how the rest of the work is read.
  • Being candid about limits helps readers judge how much weight each finding can carry.
